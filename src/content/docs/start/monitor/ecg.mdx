## Start with one beat

Look at several beats on the ECG row. In a typical sinus beat, a small **P wave** comes first as the upper chambers activate. The sharp **QRS complex** marks the activation of the lower chambers. After QRS comes the flatter **ST segment**, then the rounded **T wave** as the lower chambers electrically reset. Pick out the QRS peaks first, then compare their width and the gaps between them. [Royal Papworth Hospital ECG guide](https://www.royalpapworth.nhs.uk/our-services/cardiology/cardiac-assessment/electrocardiogram-ecg)

| What you see in the game | What it can suggest |
| --- | --- |
| Regular timing, a small wave, a narrow spike, then a rounded wave | **Sinus rhythm**. The same shape can run faster or slower. Read the rate with the patient's symptoms. |
| Very fast, regular, narrow spikes with the P waves hard to see | A common game pattern for **supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)**. Check the pulse and pressure. |
| Uneven spacing with no consistent P waves | A clue to **atrial fibrillation (AF)**. The finger probe can count fewer pulses than the ECG counts beats. |
| A run of fast, wide beats | Consider **ventricular tachycardia (VT)**. In the game it can turn upward, downward, look notched, or form a connected V–U–V shape. |
| Chaotic activity with no clear separate beats | A clue to **ventricular fibrillation (VF)**. HR may show dashes. Check the patient and pulse promptly. |
| A readable near-flat line with no pulse | Consider **asystole**. Verify the leads and look for **RESTORING** after a shock before interpreting the trace. |
| An organized ECG with no wrist pulse | **Pulseless electrical activity (PEA)** is possible. Electrical activity can continue even when the heart fails to move enough blood. Follow the in-game resuscitation prompt. |

For fast rhythms, check the wrist, current pressure when available, and the bedside hint. The mod's shock modes and eligibility depend on the rhythm and situation. ## Look at the ST segment when chest pain is concerning

Find the short section between the QRS spike and T wave. A rise above the baseline is a clue worth investigating. When the patient has concerning chest symptoms, use the game's **12-lead** if fitted, even if the bedside ST segment looks flat. The printout views the heart from several angles. If symptoms change, collect a fresh printout and compare capture times.
