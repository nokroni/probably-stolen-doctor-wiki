## Assess and act

Use **Body exam** at the bottom when you have time to examine a stable patient. After **2.5 seconds**, the chart updates and **Exam findings** brings the recorded observations together. Select a body part to read its finding and see its available actions. On critical referrals, the site card shows the newest bedside finding first. After treatment, a new exam lets you compare changes.

For an urgent problem, use the available action as soon as the bedside supports it. The boxed **Me** thought at lower left suggests what to look at next. It can point out a fresh reading or action the bedside is waiting for.

| Select on the body chart | Actions to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Head | Fit the bag and mask or take a temperature. |
| Chest | Connect ECG leads, start manual CPR, or place both defibrillator pads on their numbered guides. Then open the defibrillator to use its controls. |
| Hand or forearm | Check a pulse. Select a hand for the finger probe or glucose check. Select a forearm for eligible medicine and IV procedures. |
| Upper arm | Fit the cuff and take a blood-pressure reading. You can return to another action while the cuff cycles. |
| Injured site | Read the finding and look for an available wound or joint procedure. The action applies to the selected body part. |

The monitor groups ECG and HR, pleth and SpO₂, and the latest cuff reading (NIBP). Extra channels depend on the fitted equipment and connected probes. An ECG beep confirms electrical activity. Use the wrist check when you need to know whether the patient has a pulse.

<Aside title="Space gives one manual compression">

Start CPR at the chest, then tap and release **Space** for each compression. The mouse stays free for leads, probes, and other tools. **Stop CPR** is in the bottom controls, including when the defibrillator is open.

</Aside>

## Reach medicines quickly

Open **Medicines** from the bedside or choose an eligible forearm action. Check the name, batch purity, and price in the confirmation before giving it. Use **Pin Quick care action** to put a frequently used action one click closer. A disabled action can point to missing stock, a required device, or a finding that needs a fresh check.

Opening the medicine tray is free. If you confirm an eligible dose, the pack and any required syringe are used even if the patient doesn't respond. You can treat first and record or revise the working diagnosis later.

## Finish the visit

| Situation | What to do in the clinic |
| --- | --- |
| The patient can recover at home | Treat the problem, complete the observation, and record a working diagnosis. Choose **Discharge patient** when it turns green. Stable minor injuries generally won't require a cuff or pulse check. |
| The patient needs care beyond the shop | Choose **Call Medbay** when the case offers transfer. Keep treating and reassessing while the crew is en route. Complete the current case’s checks and use **Hand over to Medbay** when the crew and patient are ready. |
| Dispatch has to be treated on site | Follow the intake instructions and continue bedside care. When a new symptom or deterioration interrupts observation, stabilize the patient again before counting a new observation period. |

Some visits require a fitted glucose sampler at booking. If that visit is already booked and the sampler goes missing before admission, you can still assess the patient with the other required equipment and supplies in place. After the body exam, select a hand. If you see **Refer without glucose test**, use it to call the crew. Choose **Cause still unclear** if the chart offers it. Finish the remaining checks and hand over once the patient is stable and the crew has arrived.

After closing the visit, **Case review** lets you compare findings, interventions, and the outcome on one timeline. Open a recorded exam to see what the body chart showed at that point.
