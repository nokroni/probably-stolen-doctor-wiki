1. Fit a monitor in the rack and connect a charged Energy Cell or an eligible power source.
2. At the chest, use **Attach ECG**. Connect the finger probe and cuff at their own body sites. The monitor itself opens the available monitoring controls.
3. Read ECG and HR together, then the pulse waveform and SpO₂. NIBP shows the last completed cuff reading. M3 and M4 can display live ABP from a connected, zeroed arterial line.
4. Use **Save ECG** on M3 or M4 with a readable signal. After a shock, wait for the sweep to return and check the pulse before drawing conclusions.

An unconnected sensor leaves its channel blank. A separate **?** next to a number marks an uncertain reading. Check the channel message and take another reading if needed.

On the M4, a powered ventilator and connected CO₂ sampling line can show EtCO₂ during spontaneous breathing as well as assisted ventilation. The medical supplier brings upgrades from their purchase grade onward.

## Items and sources

| Item | Grade | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M2 bedside monitor | I | Starter monitor with live ECG, pulse oximetry and on-demand cuff pressure after connecting the appropriate leads and sensors. |
| M3 diagnostic monitor | II | Adds short ECG strip capture and memory. Supports live ABP with the arterial transducer and diagnostic ECG with the 12-lead recorder. |
| M4 critical care monitor | III | Includes the M3 capabilities and adds automatic cuff cycles, EtCO₂, and a continuous temperature readout with the corresponding equipment. Has a native module bay. |

## M4 modules

- **Performance** shortens a manual or automatic cuff measurement from 6 seconds to 4 at +50%. The automatic cuff still attempts a cycle about every 20 seconds.
- **Efficiency** adjusts the draw per cuff measurement and per accumulated minute of active monitoring. Each has a base cost of 1 unit.
- **Quality** shortens the wait for pleth, pulse rate, and SpO₂ once the finger probe has good contact and detects a usable pulse. At +50%, the wait drops from 3 seconds to 2.
