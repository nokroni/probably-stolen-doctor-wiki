Start with any immediate threat to breathing or circulation. Use **Body exam** when you can, then select the indicated body site or question. The **Me** box can point to the next useful game action.

  <p>Ask about meals, movement, breathing and onset. Check the chest and use 12-lead equipment where available.</p>

  <summary>Breathlessness, wheeze, hoarseness, weak chest rise</summary>
  <p>Check both sides of the chest, the exposure history and any fitted circuit. Compare a valid SpO₂ or EtCO₂ signal with the patient.</p>
  <p><a href="../breathing/">Breathing →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Cough, fever, chills, or a hot swollen wound</summary>
  <p>Take temperature, examine the chest or source, and check circulation if the patient looks unwell.</p>
  <p><a href="../infection/">Infection →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Sudden headache, loss of grip, speech change, or brief fainting</summary>
  <p>Ask when the change started. Compare speech, smile, arms and fingertip glucose. Recheck a persistent deficit after any correction.</p>
  <p><a href="../neurologic/">Neurologic symptoms →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Vomiting, diarrhea, flank pain or lower belly pain</summary>
  <p>Ask where the pain started and whether it moved. Examine the abdomen and distinguish widespread cramping from focal tenderness.</p>
  <p><a href="../abdominal/">Abdominal pain →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Sweating, tremor, confusion, cramps, unusual temperature</summary>
  <p>Check glucose or temperature, read the packet or exposure history, and check chemistry when that result is needed.</p>
  <p><a href="../metabolic-exposure/">Metabolism and exposure →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Blood on clothing, cut, heat injury, deformity or movement pain</summary>
  <p>Expose the affected site on the body chart. Look for bleeding, loose debris, a joint or bone problem and distal circulation.</p>
  <p><a href="../wounds/">Wounds and limb injuries →</a></p>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Weak pressure or cool hands after a major impact</summary>
  <p>Recheck perfusion and scan the indicated region. A small visible wound can coexist with internal bleeding.</p>
  <p><a href="../major-trauma/">Major trauma →</a></p>

## When the monitor is the first clue

- **ECG flat or missing.** Check the leads and compare the wrist. During a shock or compression the trace can be interrupted or distorted. An attached, readable flat trace with no pulse belongs in [Heart rhythms and arrest](../rhythms/).
- **Low SpO₂, low or high ABP.** Check the sensor and compare chest rise or the pulse. Where available, use a fresh cuff to compare pressure. Blood loss can still worsen circulation while the SpO₂ number looks adequate.
- **Short, low, high, or absent EtCO₂.** Short waves prompt a left-to-right chest check. A low plateau with regular breaths prompts a circulation check. A high number with slow breathing prompts a ventilation check. For no breath reading, check the connected line and the patient's movement first.

If findings conflict, repeat the check that gives a reliable current value and keep any unsettled diagnosis provisional. See [Procedure reference](../../procedures/) for control locations.
