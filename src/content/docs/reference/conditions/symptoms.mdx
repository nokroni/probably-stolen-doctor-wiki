If the patient collapsed, check the wrist and chest movement first. [Heart rhythms and cardiac arrest](../rhythms/) covers what the game offers when the pulse is absent. If the chest barely rises but a pulse is still felt, start with [Breathing problems](../breathing/).

## From the patient

Open the closest match below. Use **Body exam** and **Exam findings** to locate the clue, then select the site or ask the follow-up question. The **Me** box can point you to the next available control.

  <p>Use <strong>Check pulse</strong> at a usable wrist, attach the ECG at the chest and measure cuff BP at a usable upper arm. Compare rate and regularity with peripheral pulse and pressure before choosing a mode.</p>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Breathlessness, wheeze, hoarseness, weak chest rise</summary>
  <p>Check both sides of the chest and the mask or circuit if fitted. Ask about smoke, dust or another exposure. Compare a usable SpO₂ or EtCO₂ trace with actual movement. Uneven rise after impact also belongs under <a href="../major-trauma/">Major trauma</a>.</p>
  <p><a href="../breathing/">Compare breathing findings →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Cough, fever, chills, or a hot swollen wound</summary>
  <p>Use <strong>Take temperature</strong> where available, then look for a source. Compare both sides of the chest for cough, open the covering on a suspect wound, or ask about urination for flank pain. Trend pressure if circulation weakens.</p>
  <p><a href="../infection/">Find the source and reassess →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Headache, dizziness or brief fainting</summary>
  <p>Ask about onset, what came just before the change and whether it has happened before. Check speech and movement at the head. A sudden peak can matter even when the first exam is reassuring.</p>
  <p><a href="../neurologic/">Compare headache and fainting findings →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Slurred speech, uneven smile, arm drift or weaker grip</summary>
  <p>Ask when they were last acting normally. Compare both sides and <strong>Check glucose</strong> at a usable fingertip if fitted. Recheck the original weakness after correction. Keep a persistent deficit in the report even if the ECG looks ordinary.</p>
  <p><a href="../neurologic/">Compare low glucose and stroke findings →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Vomiting, diarrhea, flank pain or lower belly pain</summary>
  <p>Ask where the pain started, whether it moved and whether they can drink. Select the abdomen. Compare diffuse cramping with a persistent tender spot. Mention flank pain and urinary symptoms together if both are present.</p>
  <p><a href="../abdominal/">Compare abdominal findings →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Sweating, tremor, confusion, cramps or unusual temperature</summary>
  <p>Read the intake for missed meals, filtration or heat and cold. Choose <strong>Check glucose</strong>, <strong>Take temperature</strong> or <strong>Check chemistry</strong> according to that clue and the fitted kit.</p>
  <p><a href="../metabolic-exposure/">Compare metabolic and temperature clues →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Drowsiness after substance use, or a power or radiation warning</summary>
  <p>Follow any on-screen isolation prompt. If there is a wrapper, badge or witness, inspect the evidence before choosing a treatment. Compare breathing and pulse with what was reported.</p>
  <p><a href="../metabolic-exposure/">Review the exposure evidence →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Cut, burn, loose debris, deformity or pain with movement</summary>
  <p>Use the exact site on the body chart. Open its covering if prompted. Check which finding changes the action and whether the chart also asks for <strong>Check circulation</strong> farther down the injured limb.</p>
  <p><a href="../wounds/">Find a wound or limb action →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Soaked clothing, a weak pulse or cool hands after a major impact</summary>
  <p>Check each site, then repeat a current pressure and scan the affected region if the option is available. A dressed limb can coexist with a deeper injury.</p>
  <p><a href="../major-trauma/">Compare bleeding and internal injuries →</a></p>
</details>

<details name="symptom" class="symptom-choice">
  <summary>Swelling, heat, or drainage around an implant</summary>
  <p>Examine the site and inspect the service port if offered. Check temperature, breathing and circulation for changes beyond the site.</p>

## When the monitor is the first clue

| Signal | What to check next in the game |
| --- | --- |
| ECG flat, missing or recovering | Select the chest to check the leads, then compare the wrist. A shock can briefly blank the signal and compressions can distort it. If a readable trace stays flat with no pulse, see [Heart rhythms and cardiac arrest](../rhythms/). |
| SpO₂ missing or low | Check probe contact and look for a usable pulse signal. Compare the value with actual chest rise. A missing number after moving the patient can require **Refit finger clip**. |
| ABP low, high or absent | Check the line and wrist. Use a fresh cuff result when available. A normal SpO₂ can accompany falling pressure or blood loss. |
| EtCO₂ waves change shape | A sloped top can point to restricted airflow. Short, fast waves can accompany shallow breathing. Compare both sides of the chest. A low plateau with visible breaths also makes pressure and pulse worth checking. |
| EtCO₂ high or no breath detected | With a high number and slow breaths, check ventilation. With no reading, look for a disconnected line, wait for a full exhalation after reconnection and check chest movement. |

When findings disagree, repeat the measurement that has a reliable signal. Keep the diagnosis provisional until the repeat assessment. [Monitoring actions](../../procedures/monitoring/)
