---
title: Chest pain
description: Compare chest wall pain, reflux, acute coronary syndrome, heart attack and suspected pulmonary embolism.
---

Use **Ask about the pain** if shown. Ask when it started, what brings it back, and whether breathing, motion or meals change it. Use **Body exam** and select the chest to distinguish focal tenderness, differences in breathing and symptoms at rest.

| Condition | Distinguishing findings in the game | Care and disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acid reflux (GERD) | Burning after meals or lying down, sour taste, even breathing. Pressing the chest does not reproduce it. | **Rest upright** when offered. Wait through observation and repeat the chest exam before discharge. |
| Chest wall strain or cough-related chest wall pain | Movement or coughing hurts, and checking one spot brings back the pain with airflow on both sides. | Reassess after rest. On a cough presentation, check temperature if requested and compare with [pneumonia](../infection/). |
| Coronary syndrome or myocardial infarction | Pressure at rest, sweating, pain toward the shoulder or upper abdomen, or worsening waves of discomfort. | With the recorder fitted, use **Acquire 12-lead** and read the regional findings. Persistent pressure still needs attention when the strip shows no regional ST elevation. Call Medbay when the chart offers transfer. |
| Suspected pulmonary embolism | Sudden pain on breathing or breathlessness after immobility, fast breaths at rest and no wheeze on exam. | File the history and chest findings. Follow the Medbay route. A clear-sounding chest does not clear this simulated visit. |
| Chest pain, cause unclear | Useful provisional choice when the evidence is incomplete and the chart offers it. | Capture and report the findings. Repeat checks if the patient changes. |

If pressure began after an impact and one side of the chest rises less, check the [chest injury findings in Major trauma](../major-trauma/). When cough accompanies chest discomfort, compare temperature and both sides of the chest under [Fever and infection](../infection/).

## Local coronary care

If you suspect **ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)**, capture a 12-lead and check which leads show ST elevation. On eligible visits from Grade III, documented regional ST elevation can unlock Blue Blood as temporary support.

On eligible Grade IV visits with the equipment fitted, take the diagnostic tracing, confirm **Heparin** at the forearm, then choose **Start reperfusion** at the chest. Keep the ECG connected and check pulse and pressure again. If the patient deteriorates, stabilize them and start a fresh observation window.
