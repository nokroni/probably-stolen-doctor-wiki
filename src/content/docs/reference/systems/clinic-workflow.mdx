## From the window to the bed

Ask what happened and when it started. The person at the window can be the patient, a person who came with them, or a customer seeking medicine for someone elsewhere. If the patient is present, read the case needs, payer, and budget before admitting them. If they are elsewhere, look for advice or a way to arrange a visit. Admission starts the bedside clock.

1. If there is an immediate problem, use the applicable support first. **Body exam** at the bottom takes 2.5 seconds and records findings across the body. Select a site to see current findings and its local actions.
2. Use the installed instruments when a finding needs a fresh reading. ECG shows electrical activity. The finger probe tracks a pulse signal and oxygen saturation. The cuff shows the last completed reading, while an arterial line follows pressure continuously after zeroing. Check the patient and the connection if a signal changes or drops out.
3. Open **Medicines** or an eligible body-site action to choose a lot. Set a **Quick care** shortcut to keep a frequently used medicine or dressing within reach. Treat urgent findings as they arise. Use **Select diagnosis** when you have enough evidence and revise it if later findings change your assessment.

The boxed **Me** thought at the lower left points to the next useful step. Hover over an unavailable action for its requirements. Active stop controls stay in the bottom row.

The ECG tick tracks detected electrical beats. Clinical alarms call attention to a problem with a monitored reading and repeat while it persists. Equipment alerts point to issues such as a disconnected line or loss of power. Poor oxygen delivery can make a patient deteriorate over time. A restored rhythm can precede recovery in blood pressure, breathing, or the pulse signal, so keep checking the patient.

## Finish care

- **Local discharge** is for a stable visit whose treatment and observation requirements are complete. A minor wound may need only a site reassessment. When the chart asks for a new reading after treatment, repeat that measurement and let it finish. A value that stays on the monitor can be from before the treatment.
- **Call Medbay** when the chart permits transfer and the patient needs care beyond what the clinic can provide. Keep providing support while the crew is en route. Complete the chart’s handover checks or choose an offered crew takeover. Some dispatches require local care or have specific transfer terms.
- If a new symptom or genuine deterioration occurs, address it and start a fresh observation period after stabilization. Recheck the patient before treating a disconnected sensor as a new symptom.
- Read the receipt and finish the visitor’s counter reply. Some stable patients can receive a take-home prescription or a follow-up slip when the chart offers it.

## Read the record

Open **Case review** after the visit, or use **Recent cases** at the empty bed. The timeline separates patient findings, treatments, equipment and crew, and disposition. Select a marker or use **Previous event** and **Next event** to read the record. Switch between **All events** and **Turning points** to see the full sequence or its main events.

An interval along the timeline shows how long support such as CPR, bag-mask support, or ventilation was active. Check diagnosis changes against the observations around them. Interrupted or blocked actions can show what prevented completion. Select a recorded body exam, then open **Exam findings**. Use **Rhythm strips** when the chart has saved ECGs. If there is an authorized **Signed ward update**, open it from the top of the timeline. The original bedside outcome remains visible in the disposition row.
